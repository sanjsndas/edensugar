    • We are Eden Sugar House

    A small kitchen journal devoted to slow baking, wild sweetness, and the quiet craft of preserving. We believe good food takes time — and that the best recipes are the ones you return to, season after season.

    EV

    Elara Voss

    Elara is a former pastry chef who traded restaurant kitchens for a quiet counter in the Hudson Valley. She is the author of our sourdough guides, preserves, and anything involving brown butter. Her philosophy: “If it isn't worth burning, it isn't worth baking.”

    When she's not testing a new galette recipe, she's probably reading old cookbooks or tending to her ever-growing collection of fermentation crocks.

    MR

    Marcus Reed

    Marcus is a fermentation nerd and weekend baker. He brought the hot sauce, the bone broth, and the scientific curiosity. He believes that the best way to learn is to make a mess, take notes, and try again.

    Marcus handles our technique deep-dives and is the reason our pantry is always full of bubbling jars. He's currently working on a series about wild yeasts.

    What guides us

    ⏳

    Slow by choice

    We prioritize flavor over speed. Long ferments, patient proofing, and the magic of time.

    🌿

    Seasonal & wild

    We cook with what's growing now, and we let wild yeasts and natural cultures do their work.

    📓

    Tested & honest

    Every recipe is tested in our home kitchen. We share failures as well as successes.

    “The kitchen is a place of quiet transformation. Flour becomes bread; fruit becomes jam; time becomes flavor.”

    — Elara Voss, from the Eden Sugar House journal

    What you'll find here

    Eden Sugar House is a living archive of our kitchen experiments. We publish long-form guides, tested recipes, and pantry wisdom. You'll find:

    • Baking — sourdough, cookies, galettes, and the occasional cake.
    • Preserving — jams, pickles, fermented hot sauce, and preserved lemons.
    • Seasonal — what to cook with the first rhubarb, the last tomatoes.
    • Pantry — vanilla extract, bone broth, and staples worth making yourself.
    • Technique — tempering chocolate, maintaining a starter, and more.

    We have no sponsors, no ads, and no fancy equipment requirements. Just honest food, made slowly.
